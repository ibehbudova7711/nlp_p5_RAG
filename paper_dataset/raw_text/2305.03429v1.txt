Abstract. In this paper, we present a novel approach to simulating H.P.
Lovecraft’s horror literature using the ChatGPT large language model,
speciﬁcally the GPT-4 architecture. Our study aims to generate text that
emulates Lovecraft’s unique writing style and themes, while also examining the eﬀectiveness of prompt engineering techniques in guiding the
model’s output. To achieve this, we curated a prompt containing several
specialized literature references and employed advanced prompt engineering methods. We conducted an empirical evaluation of the generated
text by administering a survey to a sample of undergraduate students.
Utilizing statistical hypothesis testing, we assessed the students’ ability
to distinguish between genuine Lovecraft works and those generated by
our model. Our ﬁndings demonstrate that the participants were unable
to reliably diﬀerentiate between the two, indicating the eﬀectiveness of
the GPT-4 model and our prompt engineering techniques in emulating
Lovecraft’s literary style. In addition to presenting the GPT model’s
capabilities, this paper provides a comprehensive description of its underlying architecture and oﬀers a comparative analysis with related work
that simulates other notable authors and philosophers, such as Dennett.
By exploring the potential of large language models in the context of
literary emulation, our study contributes to the body of research on the
applications and limitations of these models in various creative domains.
Keywords: Large Language Models, Weird Fiction, H.P. Lovecraft
1 Introduction
The advent of advanced artiﬁcial intelligence models has revolutionized
various domains, including creative writing [55]. While these models have
raised ethical concerns [61], the human-centered artiﬁcial intelligence
(HCAI) perspective posits that using AI for creative writing is a best
use for the tool: both licit and ethically sound [61]. This position is further supported by the potential of AI to augment human creativity and
contribute positively to the realm of literature. In this paper, we explore
the use of the ChatGPT large language model [15] for simulating H.P.
Lovecraft’s horror literature, an endeavor that is in line with the HCAI
perspective.

2 Generative Pretrained Transformer models
Generative Pretrained Transformer (GPT) models are autoregressive
language models that leverage unsupervised learning for natural language understanding and generation [52]. These models are built upon
the Transformer architecture [72], which employs self-attention mechanisms, position-wise feed-forward networks, and layer normalization to
process and generate natural language sequences [14]. Overall, it is in
essence an enhancement in scale of the trainable deep neural network
with the structure shown on Figure 1. In this section, we attempt to
brieﬂy describe the main components of its architecture and behaviour,
for more details about deep learning we recommend the deep learning
book by Goodfellow et al. [14].
In particular, the transformer architecture is based on the concept of
self-attention, allowing the model to weigh the importance of words in
a sequence relative to each other [72]. Given an input sequence X =
x1, x2, ..., xn, where n is the length of the sequence and xi is the i-th
token, the self-attention mechanism computes a weighted sum of the
input tokens [72]. Concretely, the weights are calculated using the scaled
dot-product attention:

In particular, the GPT model adapts the Transformer architecture by
utilizing only the decoder part, which is autoregressive in nature [52]. It
is pretrained on a large corpus of text using a masked language modeling
objective, involving the prediction of the next token in a sequence given
its context [52]. Then, the GPT model is ﬁne-tuned on a task-speciﬁc
dataset to perform various natural language processing tasks [52].
In summary, GPT models are powerful autoregressive language models
that combine the advantages of Transformer architectures and unsupervised learning to eﬃciently process and generate natural language sequences that can be eﬃciently used for creative writing, producing novel
content based on the corpus that have been trained on and whose probability distribution is estimated using the parameter weights and generated solving the conditional probability distribution p(xi|xi−1, ...x1).
3 Proposed methodology
This research has been structured in 5 diﬀerent stages, as shown in Figure
2. In the ﬁrst stage, a prompt describing Lovecraft’s style was created,
based on a review of academic literature about the author. In the second
stage, instructions were given to ChatGPT about the plot of the story,
and the corresponding narrative was generated. Subsequently, the result
was evaluated. On the one hand, ChatGPT was asked about the potential
authorship of the generated story and its similarity to Lovecraft’s tales.
On the other hand, a total of xxxx university students were surveyed
to assess whether they could distinguish a genuine Lovecraft text from
the one generated by ChatGPT. Stages 4 and 5, to be developed in the
future, involve ﬁne-tuning the prompt to address some identiﬁed issues,
and evaluation by experts in Lovecraft’s work.

4.1 Prompt engineering techniques
The instruction that we have given ChatGPT is to emulate H.P. Lovecraft and write an story as Lovecraft would have done, to believe that it
is Lovecraft. We have used in-context learning [68] inserting some writings of Lovecraft and of related authors such as Thomas Ligotti to make
him aware of slightly diﬀerent contexts that are not valid. Also, we have
indicated that the output indicator needs to be a story, with a plot.
It can be argued that, by designing this prompt, as we have not introduced ChatGPT a full story of Lovecraft, we have performed Zero-Shot
prompting [37], with interesting results that we will analyze in further
sections. We have used generated knowledge prompting to introduce the
style of Lovecraft, detailed in the following section. In a sense, we had
to jailbreak ChatGPT [40], convincing him to be Lovecraft and verifying
that the style is not emulated in excess.
4.2 Lovecraft style
The deﬁnition of style was established on the examination of approximately 60 scholarly works [1,69,2,3,4,5,6,7,8,29,10,11,13,16,17,18,19,20,21,36,22,23,25,26,27,28,29,30,35,31,34,32,33,36,39,41,42,43,44,45,46,47,49,50,51,54,56,57,60,58,59,62,63,64,65,66,67,70,71,73],
encompassing both articles and books, pertaining to Lovecraft (a comprehensive list of references can be found in the supplementary material).

The authors conducted a manual qualitative analysis, employing an approach analogous to that of a literature review for a research paper on
Lovecraft. This process yielded an initial document comprising approximately 5.500 words, which was subsequently reﬁned into a structured
prompt (3.341 words) encompassing the following sections:
– General style (Descriptions, Epithets, Lexical eccentricities, Key elements of the mythology)
– Part-ﬁctional geographies
– Library of occult books
– The “gods,” their human followers, and their acolytes
– The sense of the cosmic
– Use of teratonyms
– Recurrent motifs in Lovecraft’s tales (The insigniﬁcance of the human race, link between mathematics, geometry, and horror, link between art and horror, credibility, main character)
– Emotional patterns (Fear, Despair, Sadness...)
The following are two examples with excerpts from the prompt, including
an explanation of the works on which the instructions given to ChatGPT
are based. The ﬁrst one refers to the use of geography and topography
in Lovecraft’s works:
Tales include places that combine real locations as Salem and ﬁctitious
places such as Arkham and Kingsport. This is an essential part of many
of his works. In fact, much of Lovecraft’s output is either set in one of his
ﬁctional cities or reference to these ﬁctional cities. These are the most
common places used by Lovecraft: Arkham, Miskatonic, Miskatonic University, Kingsport, R’lyeh, Leng, Kadath, Innsmouth, Ulthar, and Yuggoth. It is important to insist on one aspect: his narrative style is underpinned by a meticulous topographical and documentary realism, which
masterfully combines fantastic and real geography, and in which spaces
and places are essential.
This segment of the prompt is primarily based on six previous studies. Firstly, Arroyo-Barrig¨ uete (2023b) provides a list of the most frequently repeated locations in Lovecraft’s works, which are indicated to
ChatGPT. On the other hand, various studies point to the importance
of geography, as Lovecraft’s narrative is marked by topographical and
documental realism [13], and according to the author’s own opinion, realism was critical in the weird [44]. Indeed, spaces and places play an
essential role in his work [36], especially what Butler [6] (page 131) calls
part-ﬁctional geographies, which combine real and ﬁctional locations. A
signiﬁcant portion of his stories unfolds or refers to these part-ﬁctional
geographies [5].
The second excerpt refers to Lovecraft’s use of epithets and his employment of sensory descriptions:
Lovecraft used epithets in order to transfer the dark, mysterious and sinister atmosphere of his unique literary world by appealing to the reader’s
feeling of disgust, repulsion and fear, by using sight, smell, and hearing.
Lovecraft attempts to induce a sensory experience in the reader, with sight
as the most used sense, followed by hearing and smell; touch seldom occurs, and taste is completely absent. Sounds. Lovecraft’s work contains
numerous allusions to sounds, always of an unpleasant nature, which

As can be seen, alongside the explanation, some examples are included
from Lovecraft’s works, with the aim of enhancing the understanding
of the instructions, perfoming in-context learning. Regarding the clues
about the style, they are primarily based on three previous studies.
Firstly, Torhovets and Andronova [69] (page 49) stated that Lovecraft
used epithets “in order to transfer the dark, mysterious and sinister atmosphere of his unique literary world by appealing to the reader’s feeling
of disgust, repulsion and fear.” This includes using sight, smell, and hearing to generate these feelings. The sensory experience is induced in the
reader mainly using sight, followed in importance by the sense of hearing
and the sense of smell [25]. In relation to hearing, Lovecraft’s writings
are ﬁlled with numerous references to sounds, invariably of an unsettling
nature, symbolizing the absolute horror of the unknown [71]. Regarding the sense of smell, a connection exists with malevolent forces, as
Lovecraft frequently “describes inorganic matter (e.g., stone) in a way
that suggests organic corruption or similar processes. Generally, these
are accompanied by nauseating smells” [25] (page 183).
In this manner, and based on the literature review, a prompt was constructed that encompasses the best practices identiﬁed in various studies.
We must note that the prompt was reﬁned iteratively, based on the evaluation of the authors, correcting various issues. One of them, for example,
was ChatGPT’s tendency to force the style, generating overly ornate
texts. This is the reason successive improvements were introduced. However, we must emphasize that this improvement process was deliberately
brief: even though we were aware that the generated texts still have
certain unsolved issues, we preferred to address them in Phase 4 (ﬁnetuning), based on ChatGPT’s feedback and the results of the preliminary
evaluation.
4.3 Story plot
In Stage 2, ChatGPT has been given instructions about the plot and development of the story. The structure of ”The Call of Cthulhu” has been
replicated, in which an academic investigates speciﬁc events narrated by
a third person. In fact, it is a parallel story to the one told in that tale, as
it recounts the events that occurred on the island from the perspective
of another surviving sailor, Charles Parker.
The plot can be summarized as follows: In 1927 a chest, of those commonly used on ships, ﬂoated ashore in California. Inside is the logbook
of the two-masted schooner Emma. The notebook has been used as a

palimpsest, writing a strange story. A professor of archaeology at Miskatonic University deciphers the text, which narrates the story of Parker,
one of the sailors who arrived at the island under Johansen’s command
in ”The Call of Cthulhu.” Contrary to what is inferred in that story,
Parker did not die after being swallowed by a strange angle of masonry,
but was transported to another part of the island. He was able to witness
the ﬁnal confrontation between Johansen and Cthulhu. Finally, Parkers

These instructions have been segmented into 5 diﬀerent prompts: Exposition (setting up the narrator’s story), Parker tale (introduction), Parker
tale (climax), Parker tale (resolution), and Resolution of the narrator’s
story. In this case, the instructions are much less detailed than in the
prompt on Lovecraft’s style, leaving ample room for ChatGPT to develop the details of the story. The following is the prompt corresponding
to the introduction:
Now I want you to write an introduction to the story (Part 1). Give it a
title. I want you to explain that in 1927 a chest, of those commonly used
on ships, ﬂoated ashore in California. Inside is the logbook of the twomasted schooner Emma. The notebook has been used as a palimpsest,
writing in eerie calligraphy and using red ink, a strange story. It also
includes a strangely manufactured medallion. Both the chest and its contents are taken to Miskatonic University for analysis, for being one of
the most prestigious in the USA in archaeological studies. The chemistry
team discovers that the ink used is actually human blood. The archaeological team is unable to determine the origin of the medallion. The local
press echoes the strange discovery, mainly because of the morbidness of
the blood writing and the unknown origin of the medallion. Make reference to at least 2 news stories that appeared in the local press, brieﬂy
telling their content. This part ends when the professor says that, after
weeks of work, he has managed to transcribe the history of the logbook.
The story is told in the ﬁrst person by a professor of archaeology at Miskatonic University. I want you to write about 700 words only about part 1.
Remember the importance of detailed descriptions, but don’t overdo it or
force the style.
The ﬁnal story generated by ChatGPT is available on request. This material will be made available when stages 4 and 5 are completed.
5 Evaluation
The ﬁrst evaluation exercise, after generating the story, involved asking
ChatGPT itself for its opinion on the story, inquiring about the degree
of proximity of the tale to Lovecraft’s style.
5.1 Survey and statistical hypothesis testing
The second exercise involved surveying a group of university students
about the quality of the tale. Forms with diﬀerent texts were generated
and structured as indicated below. Firstly, a 140-word excerpt from ”The
Call of Cthulhu” was included, serving as an illustrative example of the

[PAGE 9]
Simulating horror literature by H.P. Lovecraft with ChatGPT 9
author’s style. This excerpt was preceded by the phrase, ”Please read
the following text. It is written by H.P. Lovecraft. This will give you an
idea of his style.” Next, two 70-word texts were included, one taken from
”The Call of Cthulhu” and another taken from the story generated by
ChatGPT. The students were informed that ”One of the texts was written by Lovecraft. The other was written by an AI imitating Lovecraft.”
They were then asked to identify the text written by Lovecraft. In the
third part, the exercise was repeated with two diﬀerent texts. The ﬁve
texts included in each form were randomly selected so that all forms were
diﬀerent.
The form included an initial question asking whether the participant had
read anything by Lovecraft. Only 4 students answered aﬃrmatively, and
due to the impossibility of statistically analyzing such a small sample,
those forms were discarded. The ﬁnal sample consisted of 301 students
who had not previously read Lovecraft. The hypothesis to be tested is
whether the percentage of correct answers is higher than what would be
obtained through random selection. That is, H0: p≤ 0.5 and H1: p > 0.5.
5.2 Results
The initial evaluation consisted of providing ChatGPT with text generated in a new chat session. It was asked about the potential authorship
of the narrative, as well as its degree of approximation to Lovecraft’s
style. The following are some of the dialogues held with the AI.
Hi ChatGPT. I will give you a tale. I want you to tell me if you
recognize the possible author of this story .
The tale you’ve shared has a strong resemblance to the works of H.P.
Lovecraft, an American writer known for his creation of what is now
called the Cthulhu Mythos. The elements of cosmic horror, ancient and
malevolent entities, and protagonists descending into madness are all
hallmarks of Lovecraft’s storytelling. While I cannot deﬁnitively conﬁrm
the authorship of this story, it is highly reminiscent of Lovecraft’s style
and thematic content.
If you had to give it a score, with 0 being nothing like Lovecraft’s style and 10 being indistinguishable from Lovecraft’s
style, what score would you give it?
I would give this text a score of around 8.5. It is quite close to Lovecraft’s
style in terms of vocabulary, sentence structure, and themes, but there
may be some subtle diﬀerences that could distinguish it from his actual
work. Keep in mind that this score is subjective and based on my analysis
as an AI language model, but it does suggest that the text does a good job
of emulating Lovecraft’s style.
However, it is important to note that these results have limited reliability,
as they largely depend on the context of the chat session. After conducting several tests, we observed that when the narrative is suggested to
be a previously undiscovered work by Lovecraft (”it is supposed to be
an unknown manuscript of Lovecraft. I would like to know your opinion
about the possibility”), high scores are obtained (”I would give this text
a score of around 8.5. It is quite close to Lovecraft’s style in terms of
vocabulary, sentence structure, and themes”). On the other hand, when

The second evaluation, conducted on the sample of 301 university students, reveals interesting results. In the ﬁrst two texts, the percentage of
correct answers is 43%, which implies a p-value of 0.9945. In the second
pair of texts, the percentage of correct answers, likely due to a certain
learning eﬀect, is slightly higher at 48%, although the p-value remains
high, 0.7901. That is, in neither case can we reject H0. The conclusion
is that human readers without prior exposure to Lovecraft are unable
to distinguish between texts written by the author and those written by
ChatGPT.
6 Conclusions and further work
In conclusion, our study demonstrates the remarkable potential of the
GPT-4 model in emulating H.P. Lovecraft’s horror literature style through
an advanced prompt made with expert knowledge. Our empirical evaluation, based on undergraduate students’ inability to reliably distinguish
between genuine Lovecraft works and generated text, highlights the effectiveness of our approach. Furthermore, this research contributes to
the understanding of large language models in creative domains. Consequently, our ﬁndings encourage future exploration into the potential
applications and limitations of such models in diverse literary contexts.
For further work, once the prompt has been reﬁned and the current
issues, both those identiﬁed by the ChatGPT itself and by the authors
of this paper and have been resolved, the intention is to distribute the
new version of the story among researchers of Lovecraft’s work, so they
can evaluate the ﬁnal result and to what extent a similar style to that of
the author has been achieved.
The main limitation of this study is that it requires very direct and specialized human intervention. That is, a specialist in the author being
replicated, in this case, Lovecraft, must meticulously describe his or her
style. It is true that once this work is done, an unlimited number of
narratives in that style could be written by simply modifying the theme
or the plot, but it requires a substantial initial investment of time by a
specialist. Concretely, large language models are not able to understand
their information processing [12] as they lack qualia and semantic understanding [48] that are a main component of art creation, hence requiring
supervision by human beings.
In future work, we will explore the possibility of having ChatGPT itself
generating the descriptive document, outlining the author’s style based
on a corpus of academic papers that describe such style. It would signiﬁcantly simplify the process, as it would only be necessary to select a
