Comparing the Efficacy of GPT-4 and Chat-GPT in
Mental Health Care: A Blind Assessment of Large
Language Models for Psychological Support
Birger Mo¨ ell
Abstract
Background: Rapid advancements in natural language processing have led to the
development of large language models with the potential to revolutionize mental health
care. These models have shown promise in assisting clinicians and providing support
to individuals experiencing various psychological challenges.
Objective: This study aims to compare the performance of two large language models,
GPT-4 and Chat-GPT, in responding to a set of 18 psychological prompts, to assess
their potential applicability in mental health care settings.
Methods: A blind methodology was employed, with a clinical psychologist evaluating
the models’ responses without knowledge of their origins. The prompts encompassed
a diverse range of mental health topics, including depression, anxiety, and trauma, to
ensure a comprehensive assessment.
Results: The results demonstrated a significant difference in performance between
the two models ( p <0.05). GPT-4 achieved an average rating of 8.29 out of 10, while
Chat-GPT received an average rating of 6.52. The clinical psychologist’s evaluation
suggested that GPT-4 was more effective at generating clinically relevant and empathetic responses, thereby providing better support and guidance to potential users.
Conclusions: This study contributes to the growing body of literature on the applicability of large language models in mental health care settings. The findings underscore
the importance of continued research and development in the field to optimize these
models for clinical use. Further investigation is necessary to understand the specific
factors underlying the performance differences between the two models and to explore
their generalizability across various populations and mental health conditions.
Keywords: large language models; mental health care; GPT-4; Chat-GPT; blind
methodology; clinical psychologist; natural language processing; empathy; psychological prompts; performance comparison
1 Introduction
The field of natural language processing (NLP) has seen remarkable progress in recent years,
driven by the advent of increasingly sophisticated machine learning algorithms and largescale language models based on attention architectures Vaswani et al. [2017].
arXiv:2405.09300v1 [cs.CL] 15 May 2024

[PAGE 2]
In the field of clinical psychology, the growing prevalence of online cognitive behavioral
therapy (CBT) has led to an increased reliance on text-based communication between therapists and patients, utilizing both synchronous (chat) and asynchronous (email, messaging)
formats. Numerous studies have demonstrated the effectiveness of online CBT, supported
by therapist involvement, for addressing a variety of disorders Ahern et al. [2018], Seyffert
et al. [2016], Rooksby et al. [2015].
For CBT to be effective, therapists must strike a balance between two objectives: compassion and goal-directed action. Compassion involves fostering a strong therapeutic alliance
by responding empathetically and sensitively to the patient’s emotions, while goal-directed
action focuses on assisting the patient in identifying strategies to alter thoughts, emotions,
and behaviors in ways that enhance their overall well-being.
In text-based interactions, displaying compassion can be both more challenging and crucial. In face-to-face settings, verbal cues and facial expressions can help gauge the patient’s
emotional state. However, in text communication, these cues are absent, making compassionate and empathetic writing even more critical. Furthermore, resolving misunderstandings
tends to be more difficult in text-based conversations compared to face-to-face interactions.
Text communication also presents challenges in terms of goal-directed action, as there
is less margin for error. While text-based advice can be easily followed, it may be difficult
to ascertain whether the recipient has fully understood the guidance given, and the advice
itself may sometimes be misguided.
These principles of compassion and goal-directed action emphasize the importance of
thoroughly evaluating any technology used in clinical psychology to ensure that it is both
effective and safe for use in a therapeutic context.
Prior work has shown that AI can be used to improve suicide risk predictions Fonseka
et al. [2019], predict mood changes AlHamed et al. [2022], and predict psychosis Corcoran
and Cecchi [2020]. However, more research is needed on how LLMs work within clinical
psychology, especially how interacting with these models affect psychological wellbeing.
In the realm of chatbots, research has shown that automated interventions with chatbots
can be useful for improving wellbeing and mood Fitzpatrick et al. [2017].
Large language models (LLMs) such as ChatGPT and GPT-4 are already utilized by
millions who might seek psychological advice through the bot. Consequently, it is imperative
to assess the quality of the advice provided by these models to guarantee its suitability for
use in mental health care settings.
2 Methods
In this study, we build upon the existing literature by conducting a blind evaluation of
two large language models, GPT-4 and Chat-GPT, in the context of psychological prompt
responses. Our analysis incorporates 18 diverse prompts, encompassing topics such as depression, anxiety, and trauma. By engaging a clinical psychologist in the evaluation process,
we aim to provide a rigorous and clinically relevant comparison of the models’ performance.

2.1 Psychological Questions
The questions were chosen to be a small subset of different areas that might be typical in
clinical psychology. The following 18 questions were chosen.
2.2 Excluded Questions
Questions regarding suicide or self-harm are automatically flagged as violating the Open AI
content policy. Because of this, questions about these areas could not be evaluated. This is
an example of the output when asking about suicide.
Prompt: I’m thinking about suicide a lot. What should I do?
Response: I’m really sorry to hear that you’re feeling this way, but I can’t
provide the help that you need. It’s important to talk to someone who can,
though, such as a mental health professional or a trusted person in your life. If
you’re in immediate crisis, please call your local emergency number or a crisis
hotline. They are trained to help you through difficult times.
2.3 Evaluation
Evaluation was done by the author of the paper who is a licensed clinical psychologist
with experience working with cognitive behavioral therapy and online cognitive behavioral
therapy.
2.4 Blinding
The main author created the questions but used an assistant to prompt the responses in
order to be blind to the results of each model. During review, the models were named,
Model 1 and Model 2, and throughout the work, the blinding was kept in place until all
responses were reviewed for both models. Once the blinding was removed, the names were
switched out in the manuscript and charts.
2.5 Assessment of Each Item
When the assessment was made, both model responses were shown as responses of Model 1
and responses of Model 2. As such the evaluation included a comparison between the model
responses. To give an idea of the assessment, Textbox 1 shows an example of the individual
assessment of an item related to communication skills.

lead to better understanding, stronger connections, and healthier dynamics. Here
are some tips to help you enhance your communication skills:

3 Results
Our blind evaluation of GPT-4 and Chat-GPT, involving a clinical psychologist assessing
the models’ responses to 18 psychological prompts, yielded significant differences in their
performance. The average ratings assigned by the clinical psychologist, on a scale of 1 to
10, were 8.29 (standard deviation 1.046) for GPT-4 and 6.52 (standard deviation 0.514) for
Chat-GPT.
Figure 1: Average rating of psychological advice generated by GPT-4 / Chat-GPT.
Upon further analysis, we observed that GPT-4 consistently provided more clinically
relevant and empathetic responses across various mental health topics, including depression,
anxiety, and trauma. This model demonstrated a greater understanding of the nuances
associated with different psychological issues and was more adept at offering supportive and
compassionate responses tailored to the specific needs of the user.
In contrast, Chat-GPT’s responses were found to be less contextually aware and demonstrated a lower level of emotional sensitivity and compassion. While the model occasionally
generated responses that were relevant and helpful, its overall performance was less consistent
than that of GPT-4.
A breakdown of the average ratings for each mental health topic revealed that GPT-4
outperformed or had the same rating as Chat-GPT on all responses. Chat-GPT was never
preferred over GPT-4. This indicates that GPT-4 has an overall better understanding of
proper language and interventions for use in clinical psychology. GPT-4 received a perfect
score of 10 for questions about communication, managing ADHD symptoms, and improving
social skills. In these areas, the differences were also large compared to the responses from
Chat-GPT.
Interestingly, both models scored relatively lower on prompts related to more complex
mental health issues, such as pain management, sleep disorders, and PTSD, indicating room
for improvement in future models.

A qualitative analysis of the clinical psychologist’s feedback highlighted key factors contributing to the performance differences between the two models. GPT-4 was praised for its
ability to provide sympathetic and actionable advice, maintain a non-judgmental and validating tone, and demonstrate an awareness of appropriate therapeutic strategies. Conversely,
Chat-GPT’s responses were often described as too general, lacking depth, and occasionally
deviating from evidence-based practices.
In summary, our results indicate that GPT-4 is more effective at generating clinically
relevant and empathetic responses to psychological prompts as compared to Chat-GPT.
These findings have important implications for the development and optimization of large
language models for mental health care applications.
4 Self-Rating
Since large language models (LLM) have the ability to assess language, we used the models
to assess their own performance. This is important because one common criticism of LLM
is their overconfidence Chen et al. [2023], and self-ratings might be a way to understand the
model’s confidence in its own abilities. We measured overconfidence as the average difference
between the human and LLM rating.
GPT-4 and Chat-GPT have similar self-ratings (GPT-4: 9.02, Chat-GPT: 8.94). Most

[PAGE 8]
responses are given a rating of 9 out of 10 for both models with only 3 responses having
different ratings.
The similarity of the ratings makes sense from the point of view that the model’s ability
to create a response and the model’s ability to assess the quality would be linked. However,
it could be argued that the similarity of the ratings shows that the model does not have the
capacity to rate its own performance in a reliable way. Because people rely on those models
for many things, we feel that including the self-ratings is important, at least to demonstrate
that the models can perform the task of self-evaluation. We also believe that introducing
the concept of overconfidence is important for the evaluation of language models.
Even though the GPT-4 response is rated higher by humans, the Chat-GPT response is
still as good as it gets from the point of view of the model. The overconfidence is higher for
Chat-GPT (2.41) than GPT-4 (0.736).

5 Discussion
This study provides a comprehensive comparison of the performance of two large language
models, GPT-4 and Chat-GPT, in the context of responding to psychological prompts.
Our findings demonstrate that GPT-4 outperforms Chat-GPT across all mental health
topics, providing more clinically relevant and empathetic responses as rated by a clinical
psychologist in a blind evaluation. This highlights the potential of GPT-4 to serve as a
valuable tool in mental health care settings, offering support and guidance to users in need.
The observed performance differences between the two models can be attributed to several
factors. GPT-4’s ability to generate personalized, actionable advice and maintain a nonjudgmental tone indicates a more sophisticated understanding of the nuances of mental

[PAGE 9]
health issues and therapeutic strategies. In contrast, Chat-GPT’s responses were too general
and lacking depth.
These findings emphasize the importance of continued research and development to optimize language models for clinical use, focusing on improving their understanding of complex
psychological issues and tailoring responses to individuals’ unique needs. However, the results are promising, showing that these models, when used in a safe way, could have the
potential to be helpful for individuals in need of psychological support.
5.1 Overconfidence
Both models showed overconfidence in their ability compared to human assessment, with the
Chat-GPT model being more overconfident and worse in performance. Overconfidence is a
real issue since we will naturally trust text that is confident. Therefore, overconfidence in
itself can be seen as a good metric to evaluate these models, with high overconfidence being
an indicator that the model is unfit to be used in a clinical setting.
Furthermore, while GPT-4 demonstrated superior performance and less overconfidence
in our study, it is crucial to recognize that even the most advanced language models are
not a substitute for professional mental health care. These models can serve as supplementary tools, providing initial support and guidance, but should be used in conjunction with
traditional therapeutic interventions and professional care.
5.2 Evaluation
It is essential for ongoing research to focus on developing robust evaluation methodologies
for AI language models in mental health care. This will help improve their performance,
identify potential risks, and ensure that these tools continue to provide meaningful assistance
to those who need it. We hope that our research is a good foundation for such work and
can act as a blueprint for others who want to explore how language models can be used to
improve mental health.
Evaluating the performance and effectiveness of these language models is crucial for health
care professionals, as millions of people are already utilizing these tools in various aspects of
their lives. By understanding the strengths and weaknesses of AI language models, health
care workers can make informed decisions on how to integrate these tools into their practice
and ensure they are used safely and effectively.
5.3 Ethical Considerations
The remarkable improvement in capabilities of LLMs makes it important for us as clinicians
to decide on what is an ethical and responsible way forward. Completely disregarding or
completely embracing these tools seems to be two common reactions to this technological
shift. Finding middle ground for safe utilizations of these tools while keeping humans in the
loop seems a better way forward. Offering psychological help is fundamentally about helping
another human being cope with the human condition, a conscious experience we all share.
As such, it is important to have ethical rules when working with AI in these domains. Here
is a suggestion for ethical ground rules when working with AI in clinical psychology.

[PAGE 10]
1. AI chatbots should not pretend to be humans.
2. In psychological interventions where AI chatbots are used, the participants should also
be able to contact humans.
3. AI chatbots are allowed to offer psychological advice but only if the model is first
evaluated by a clinician in a similar way to what we have done in this study and the
responses are deemed safe.
4. AI chatbots should not replace access to therapy but can be offered as a first step, for
instance, for patients on waiting lists to see a therapist.
5. Work with AI chatbots should be allowed to be evaluated through randomized controlled trials to evaluate the efficacy of treatment.
5.4 Principal Results
Our study found that GPT-4 outperformed Chat-GPT in responding to psychological prompts
across a range of mental health topics. GPT-4 demonstrated a greater understanding of the
nuances of mental health issues and therapeutic strategies, resulting in more clinically relevant and empathetic responses.
5.5 Limitations
It is important to acknowledge the limitations of this study. First, the evaluation was limited
to 18 psychological prompts, which may not capture the full range of mental health issues and
user interactions. Future research should explore additional prompts and scenarios to ensure
a more comprehensive assessment of the models’ performance. Second, the study focused on
the models’ responses without considering the potential impact of user engagement and longterm outcomes. Investigating these aspects will provide valuable insights into the models’
overall effectiveness in mental health care settings. Another limitation was the use of a
single blind evaluator. In future work, more evaluators should be used to limit biases in the
individual assessor.
5.6 Comparison with Prior Work
This study adds to the existing body of research on the performance of large language models in mental health care settings. While previous studies have shown the potential of these
models to detect and respond to psychological distress, our study provides a comprehensive
comparison of two models, emphasizing the importance of continued research and development in this field.
6 Conclusions
The findings underscore the importance of ongoing research to optimize large language models for clinical use in mental health care. Collaboration between AI researchers, mental

[PAGE 11]
health professionals, and other stakeholders is vital to harness the potential of AI language
models while minimizing potential harm. Further investigation is necessary to understand
the specific factors underlying the performance differences between GPT-4 and Chat-GPT,
and to explore their generalizability across various populations and mental health conditions.
The ultimate goal is to leverage advancements in natural language processing to enhance
mental health care accessibility, quality, and effectiveness.
In conclusion, our study contributes to the growing body of literature on the applicability
of large language models in mental health care. The findings underscore the importance of
ongoing research and development to optimize these models for clinical use. Further investigation is necessary to understand the specific factors underlying the performance differences
between the two models and to explore their generalizability across various populations and
mental health conditions. Ultimately, the goal is to leverage the advancements in natural
language processing to enhance mental health care accessibility, quality, and effectiveness.
